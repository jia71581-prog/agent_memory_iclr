**The attribution control is the reference that matters.** `qc_no_lifecycle` (BM25 top-20 raw turns, no view) scores *below* Full (0.806 vs 0.818): moving from k=30 to k=20 costs about 1.2 pp on its own. **The ablation that matters is null.** Removing query conditioning entirely (`qc_no_qc`: the view is always rendered in historical mode, for every question) costs 0.4 pp on 500 questions, b=13 vs c=11, p=0.84, CI [-0.014, +0.024]. ### 7c. Verdict

**The success line is met in aggregate and on the neutral constraint, and fails on the one subtype it was written for.**

- current-state (KU, Reading A): **+1.4 pp, target +3 pp -> NOT MET**, and as shown above the test is structurally underpowered (p could not have fallen below 0.0625).
- current-state (Reading B, `current`-labelled queries): +12.9 pp over the best baseline, target cleared numerically, but n=31, b=5, c=1, p=0.22 - **not statistically supported**, and identical to the no-query-conditioning ablation (b=1, c=1), so the gain is not attributable to the mechanism.
- historical/temporal (TR): **+2.4 pp, target +2 pp -> MET numerically** (109/127 vs 106/127, i.e. three questions), **but p=0.65**; three flipped questions is not a result. Reading B (`historical`-labelled, n=44): +2.3 pp, p=1.00.
- neutral degradation <1 pp: **MET in substance, with one literal violation.** The only negative cell is SSU at -1.6 pp, which is a single question (62/64 vs 63/64) and so nominally breaches a literal 1-pp threshold while being indistinguishable from noise (b=0, c=1). SSA +1.8, SSP +20.0, MS +9.9 are all positive. No neutral subtype degrades materially.
- aggregate: +4.8 pp over Full on 500 (p=0.0056, Holm 0.011) and +6.0 pp over the attribution control (p=7.3e-05, Holm 2.9e-04) are real and survive correction.

The uncomfortable summary for the writeup: **the method wins, but not for the reason the plan predicted.** The gain is carried by single-session-preference and multi-session - subtypes the design did not target - while knowledge-update and temporal-reasoning, the subtypes the whole lifecycle/query-conditioning story is built on, show sub-noise movements. Combined with the null query-conditioning ablation, the defensible claim is narrower than the plan's: *a compact, deterministically constructed lifecycle view over retrieved turns improves aggregate QA accuracy at fixed retrieval budget*, with the query-conditioning component currently unsupported.

| FIFO eviction (MemoryOS-style) | 226 (48.1%) | 26 | 0.009 | 0.545 |
| BM25-only k=20 (attribution control) | 15 (3.2%) | 13 | 0.000 | 0.826 |
| **QC-Lifecycle (ours)** | 4 (0.9%) | 12 | 0.250 | 0.873 |
| - query conditioning (view always historical) | 7 (1.5%) | 15 | 0.000 | 0.875 |
| - raw turns (view only) | 78 (16.6%) | 18 | 0.000 | 0.564 |

The lifecycle view cuts unnecessary refusals by ~85%: Full says 'I don't know' on 28 of 470 answerable questions, ours on 4. A refusal on an answerable question is essentially always scored wrong (Full 0.036, Time-decay and the attribution control 0.000). **18 of the 47 questions ours wins over Full (38%) are questions where Full refused.** The same shift is what costs us the abstention subset in section 4 - one mechanism, two signs.

Restricting to the questions where the reference arm *attempted* an answer (a baseline-defined subgroup, so the split does not depend on our arm; this is a diagnostic, not a pre-registered test):

| comparison | n attempted | ours | ref | delta | 95% CI | b | c | p | vs. full-sample delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ours vs full | 442 | 0.880 | 0.860 | +0.0204 | [-0.011, +0.052] | 29 | 20 | 0.2529 | was +0.0574 |
| ours vs qc_no_lifecycle | 455 | 0.884 | 0.826 | +0.0571 | [+0.029, +0.086] | 37 | 11 | 2.22e-04 | was +0.0681 |
| ours vs decay | 442 | 0.885 | 0.839 | +0.0452 | [+0.011, +0.079] | 38 | 18 | 0.0105 | was +0.0787 |

**This is the finding most likely to decide the paper's fate.** Against the strongest baseline (Full), removing the refusal effect collapses the advantage from +0.0574 (p=0.0013) to **+0.0204, b=29 vs c=20, p=0.2529, CI [-0.011, +0.052] - not significant.** On the same subgroup KU is exactly tied (+0.000, b=2, c=2) and TR is slightly negative (-0.008, b=7, c=8). In other words, about 65% of the headline gain over the no-forgetting baseline is the method making the model *willing to answer*, not the method making the model *answer better*.

Against the attribution control the picture is different and holds up: +5.7 pp, b=37, c=11, p=2e-04 on the 455 attempted questions, with multi-session +13.8 pp (b=17, c=1, p=1e-04). Against Time-decay it also holds: +4.5 pp, p=0.0105.

Consequences for the writeup:

1. The defensible headline is **ours vs the same-retrieval control** (+6.0 pp, p=7.3e-05, robust to the refusal decomposition), not 'ours vs full memory'.
2. A reviewer can cheaply test whether an answer-forcing prompt on the baseline erases the Full comparison. Run that arm before submission: same Full baseline, prompt instructed not to refuse unless the answer is genuinely absent. If it closes the gap, the honest claim shrinks; if it does not, this section becomes evidence rather than a liability.
3. Report refusal rate and abstention accuracy as first-class numbers. The view trades calibrated refusal for coverage; that trade is a finding, and hiding it in an aggregate invites the reviewer to find it instead.
